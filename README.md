Modelled West Coast blowout — oil reached shore in 75% of simulations
https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-11-24-modelled-west-coast-blowout-oil-reached-shore-in-75-of-simulations/

"Scientists working with the conservation organisation WILDTRUST simulated a 20-day blowout at a well site about 60km off Cape Point 200 times, each starting on a different date, and in 75% of the runs, oil reached the shore above a set level of contamination. For spills starting between April and September, that figure rose to 98%.

These are not the odds of a blowout happening. They show how likely oil would be to reach the coast if one did.

The findings were presented at a WILDTRUST workshop in Cape Town last week, as companies line up to drill off the West Coast – and as the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday without a deal. The European Union, Panama and the Marshall Islands, among others, objected to a draft that did not mention fossil fuels.

“We can’t put out the fire while pouring fuel on it,” UN Secretary-General António Guterres said in a statement afterwards.

At home, about 90% of the ocean around South Africa is leased for oil and gas exploration, according to WILDTRUST.

TotalEnergies has authorisation to drill an exploration well 188km offshore in the Deep Western Orange Basin, with up to nine more if it finds oil or gas. The Aukotowa co-operative, which represents fishers in Port Nolloth, has challenged the authorisation alongside The Green Connection and Natural Justice. Judgment is expected soon. Shell has also been authorised to drill up to five wells in its Northern Cape Ultra Deep block, while environmental groups have appealed.

Dr Stephen Holness of Nelson Mandela University mapped what lies in a spill’s path: nearly 1,000 ecological features, along with fisheries, tourism, mariculture and 136 rural coastal communities in four provinces that depend directly on the ocean.

A spill could close fishing grounds or make fish unsafe to eat or sell for months, years or, in some cases, decades, Harris said.
